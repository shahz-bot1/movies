100 scenes that made cinema laugh

From a mirror without glass to a fanny pack with perfect aim: the set pieces, exchanges and gags that turned laughter into craft.

01Duck SoupThe mirror scene19331930s
The setup

Rufus T. Firefly (Groucho Marx) returns home to find his "reflection" behaving oddly — because there is no mirror, only a doorway, and the figure is Pinky (Harpo Marx) in an identical nightshirt, nightcap and greasepaint moustache. What follows is cinema's most perfect mime duet: Groucho tips his hat, Harpo tips his; Groucho dances, Harpo dances, matched beat for beat. Then the symmetry deliberately cracks — impossible spins, dropped poses — and Chico Marx strolls in wearing the same disguise, tripling the chaos as all three shuffle positions to bamboozle Groucho.

Why it works

Harpo's stone-faced precision makes the impossible feel logical, and each broken reflection tops the last.

The impact

the most imitated gag in film history, from I Love Lucy to Bugs Bunny. Note: the mirror gag predates the film (vaudeville tradition; Max Linder filmed one in 1921) — this is the definitive version. Release-era response is undocumented; today, its timing still plays.

02Airplane!"Don't call me Shirley"19801980s
The setup

The brilliance is structural: Airplane! plays a disaster movie dead straight, so Nielsen — a serious actor — delivers idiotic wordplay with total conviction while the stakes are life and death. The line lands because nobody on screen knows it's a joke.

The impact

AFI-listed among the great movie quotes; it made Nielsen, at 54, the defining deadpan comedian of his generation and the template for the entire ZAZ style.
