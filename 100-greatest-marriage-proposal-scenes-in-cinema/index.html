100 Greatest Marriage Proposal Scenes in Cinema

Ranked by emotional power, originality, cinematic craft, cultural impact and lasting audience love — from whispered questions to spectacular public declarations.

01Love ActuallyJamie (Colin Firth) → Aurélia (Lúcia Moniz)On-screen proposal20032000s
The scene

After a courtship conducted across a language barrier, Jamie travels to Marseille, France and, before Aurélia's entire family and a restaurant full of patrons, proposes in halting, painstakingly learned Portuguese. She answers in English: yes.

The impact

Widely regarded as the most beloved proposal in modern romantic comedy — the embodiment of the film's love-transcends-language thesis and a cornerstone of its status as a perennial holiday favorite.

02Jerry MaguireJerry Maguire (Tom Cruise) → Dorothy Boyd (Renée Zellweger)On-screen proposal19961990s
The scene

Jerry asks Dorothy to marry him while his life and career are still uncertain; she accepts, and they marry before his professional comeback is secure. The film’s famous support-group reunion comes later, after their marriage is strained: Jerry tells her, "You complete me," and Dorothy answers, "You had me at hello."

The impact

Two of the most quoted lines in film history from a Best Picture nominee; Cuba Gooding Jr. won the Supporting Actor Oscar, and "you complete me" entered the language permanently.

03Pride & PrejudiceFitzwilliam Darcy (Matthew Macfadyen) → Elizabeth Bennet (Keira Knightley)On-screen proposal20052000s
The scene

The rain-soaked first proposal — a torrent of feeling that insults as much as it confesses — is rejected on grounds of arrogance and interference; the dawn renewal, stripped of pride, is accepted.

The impact

One of period-drama cinema's most iconic scenes; its power is passion colliding with principle, a proposal that fails precisely because of how it's delivered.

0450 First DatesHenry Roth (Adam Sandler) → Lucy Whitmore (Drew Barrymore)Proposal-adjacent20042000s
The scene

The film never shows Henry proposing. Instead, its Arctic epilogue reveals that Henry and Lucy are already married and raising a daughter; Lucy wakes aboard their boat, relearns their life through a videotape, and chooses her family anew that morning.

The impact

The marriage reveal subverts the conventional proposal payoff and reframes commitment around choosing someone every single day — the film’s most enduring romantic idea.

05The ProposalAndrew Paxton (Ryan Reynolds) → Margaret Tate (Sandra Bullock)On-screen proposal20092000s
The scene

At the office, after the sham engagement collapses, Andrew proposes for real: "Margaret, will you marry me? Because I'd like to date you."

The impact

The "date you" button inverts the entire premise — wanting the beginning, not the shortcut — and became the signature, widely quoted line of one of 2009's biggest rom-com hits.

06Sex and the CityJohn "Mr. Big" Preston (Chris Noth) → Carrie Bradshaw (Sarah Jessica Parker)On-screen proposal20082000s
The scene

In the walk-in closet, Big proposes with her blue Manolo Blahnik shoe instead of a ring. Carrie accepts, closing the wound of being jilted at the altar.

The impact

Instantly iconic — a fashion-culture moment as much as a romantic one — resolving six seasons and a film of heartbreak in a single image.

07When Harry Met SallyHarry Burns (Billy Crystal) → Sally Albright (Meg Ryan)Proposal-adjacent19891980s
The scene

On New Year's Eve, Harry catalogues everything he loves about Sally, ending: "when you realize you want to spend the rest of your life with somebody, you want the rest of your life to start as soon as possible." Marriage is confirmed only in the epilogue interview — there is no explicit on-screen ask.

The impact

Borderline: no explicit proposal. Included because it is arguably the most quoted romantic speech ever filmed, and the template for every grand-declaration finale since.

08Sweet Home AlabamaAndrew Hennings (Patrick Dempsey) → Melanie Carmichael (Reese Witherspoon)On-screen proposal20022000s
The scene

Andrew stages a private after-hours visit to Tiffany & Co., where Melanie selects her own ring. She accepts, swept into the fairy-tale New York life.

The impact

The early-2000s rom-com's signature image of luxury romance — dramatically, the gilded alternative, which is exactly why Melanie's return to Alabama reads as artifice rejected for authenticity.

09Crazy Rich AsiansNick Young (Henry Golding) → Rachel Chu (Constance Wu)On-screen proposal20182010s
The scene

After Rachel refuses Nick's first proposal — she will not make him choose between her and his family — she boards a New York-bound flight with her mother, Kerry. Nick rushes aboard before departure and proposes again with Eleanor's emerald ring; Rachel's acceptance becomes their reconciliation, with the heirloom now offered on new terms.

The impact

The defining proposal of late-2010s cinema, replayed endlessly in clip culture — a rom-com beat turned into a statement about family, class, and belonging, and a landmark for Asian-American representation.

10Forrest GumpForrest Gump (Tom Hanks) → Jenny Curran (Robin Wright) — declined; then Jenny → Forrest — acceptedOn-screen proposal19941990s
The scene

Forrest proposes simply — "I'm not a smart man… but I know what love is" — and Jenny gently declines. Later, she proposes to him, and he accepts.

The impact

A rare major-film proposal built around a "no," in a Best Picture winner — the scene's humility is why it endures.

11MoonstruckRonny Cammareri (Nicolas Cage) → Loretta Castorini (Cher)On-screen proposal19871980s
The scene

At the family kitchen table, after Loretta's engagement to Johnny ends, Ronny proposes with Johnny's returned ring. She accepts amid a family toast.

The impact

Cher won Best Actress and the screenplay won the Original Screenplay Oscar; the scene is the warm, operatic heart of the film.

12A Star Is BornJackson Maine (Bradley Cooper) → Ally (Lady Gaga)On-screen proposal20182010s
The scene

During a visit to his friend George “Noodles” Stone’s home, Jackson and Ally are together when Noodles suggests that the pair should marry. Jackson turns the idea into an immediate proposal, improvising a ring from a guitar string; Ally accepts, and they marry that same day.

The impact

The guitar-string ring became one of the decade's most discussed proposal images — romance stripped of wealth and spectacle, retroactively the film's emotional peak before tragedy closes in.

13Runaway BrideMaggie Carpenter (Julia Roberts) → Ike Graham (Richard Gere)On-screen proposal19991990s
The scene

Gender-flipped: Maggie seats Ike, kneels, and asks, "I love you, Homer Eisenhower Graham. Will you marry me?" — followed by her "I guarantee that we'll have tough times…" vows speech.

The impact

The kneeling reversal was the film's thesis in miniature — the runaway bride finally running toward something — delivered in script-sourced dialogue.

14Pretty WomanEdward Lewis (Richard Gere) → Vivian Ward (Julia Roberts)Proposal-adjacent19901990s
The scene

Edward climbs the fire escape with flowers to rescue Vivian; she delivers the film's thesis: "She rescues him right back." There is no marriage proposal of any kind — it is a romantic reunion.

The impact

Borderline: no proposal. Included as one of cinema's most imitated romantic climaxes, the fairy-tale image that defined a decade of rom-coms.

15Notting HillAnna Scott (Julia Roberts) → William Thacker (Hugh Grant) — a reverse proposalProposal-adjacent19991990s
The scene

In William’s bookshop, Anna pleads to resume the relationship: "I'm also just a girl, standing in front of a boy, asking him to love her." He refuses, then reconsiders and races to her press conference, where she says she will remain in Britain "indefinitely"; a wedding appears only in the closing montage. No on-screen proposal.

The impact

Borderline: no on-screen proposal. Included for a speech quoted at real weddings for a quarter-century.

16The Wedding SingerRobbie Hart (Adam Sandler) → Julia Sullivan (Drew Barrymore)Proposal-adjacent19981990s
The scene

On the Vegas-bound plane, Robbie performs "Grow Old with You" to stop Julia's wedding to Glen; she chooses him. The proposal exists only in lyrical form — even the draft screenplay contains no explicit ask.

The impact

Borderline: no verbal proposal. Included because the song-as-proposal became the film's signature and a real-world wedding staple.

17A Walk to RememberLandon Carter (Shane West) → Jamie Sullivan (Mandy Moore)On-screen proposal20022000s
The scene

After fulfilling her telescope-and-comet wish, Landon asks: "Will you do something for me?" … "Will you marry me?" Jamie accepts, both knowing her time is short.

The impact

The emotional crest of a generational weepie — no spectacle, just promises kept — and the reason the film endures.

18While You Were SleepingJack Callaghan (Bill Pullman) → Lucy Moderatz (Sandra Bullock)On-screen proposal19951990s
The scene

Jack slides the ring through her CTA token tray, pays his fare, steps into the booth: "Marry me?" She says yes, the whole family watching.

The impact

The token-tray staging made it one of the decade's most distinctive proposals and confirmed Bullock as the era's rom-com star.

19Leap YearDeclan O'Callaghan (Matthew Goode) → Anna Brady (Amy Adams)On-screen proposal20102010s
The scene

Anna returns to Dingle offering a future with "no plans." Declan, who has resisted her all film, produces his mother's Claddagh ring and flips the premise — he wants plans, with her — proposing on the cliffs above the sea.

The impact

The Claddagh-ring beat gave the film its most quoted romantic image and a durable place in "proposal destination" lore, anchoring Leap Day rewatches every four years.

20About TimeTim Lake (Domhnall Gleeson) → Mary (Rachel McAdams)On-screen proposal20132010s
The scene

Tim rushes home to Mary and proposes in bed — no staging, no ring shot, just the question. Sleepy, surprised, delighted, she says yes.

The impact

Richard Curtis's low-key staging made it the template for the "anti-spectacle" proposal: intimacy over production, beloved precisely because it looks like real life.

21Gone with the WindRhett Butler (Clark Gable) → Scarlett O'Hara (Vivien Leigh)On-screen proposal19391930s
The scene

Rhett teases — "So, you see I shall have to marry you" — drops to his knees, then turns earnest: "This is an honorable proposal of marriage made at what I consider a most opportune moment. I can't go all my life waiting to catch you between husbands." … "Say you're going to marry me. Say yes." / "Yes."

The impact

"An honorable proposal of marriage" is among cinema's most quoted proposal lines — the archetypal scoundrel-goes-sincere, at the heart of the Best Picture phenomenon.

22The GodfatherMichael Corleone (Al Pacino) → Kay Adams (Diane Keaton)On-screen proposal19721970s
The scene

Over a year after Sicily, Michael finds Kay teaching in New Hampshire and walks her down a country road: "I came here because I need you — because I care for you —" / "Please stop it, Michael…" / "— because — I want you to marry me…" She goes with him.

The impact

The hinge of Michael's transformation — the war hero binding Kay to the life he once shunned — and the emotional center of the Best Picture winner.

23A Star Is BornNorman Maine (James Mason) → Esther Blodgett / Vicki Lester (Judy Garland)On-screen proposal19541950s
The scene

In a recording studio: "I wasn't listening. I was thinking about another kind of arrangement." / "Another kind?" / "Huh. Domestic. Will you marry me?" / "No, thank you." She lists his faults; he offers to become dependable; she relents before the whole crew: "That's much too public a proposal… for me to say no to. I accept." Two technicians have recorded it all.

The impact

Among Garland's most beloved scenes — funny, tender, aching; both stars were Oscar-nominated and won Golden Globes.

24His Girl FridayWalter Burns (Cary Grant) → Hildy Johnson (Rosalind Russell) — a remarriage proposalOn-screen proposal19401940s
The scene

With the Earl Williams crisis over, Walter announces to his editor, Hildy listening tearfully: "We're gonna get married." / "Oh! Can we go on a honeymoon this time?" He promises the Niagara Falls honeymoon they never had — then reroutes it through a strike in Albany. "We'll honeymoon in Albany…"

The impact

A proposal delivered as brisk newsroom declaration — one of screwball comedy's most quoted endings (AFI's 100 Years…100 Laughs #19; National Film Registry).

25Rocky IIRocky Balboa (Sylvester Stallone) → Adrian Pennino (Talia Shire)On-screen proposal19791970s
The scene

Snowy day, Philadelphia Zoo, tiger enclosure. "I was wondering if you wouldn't mind marrying me very much." / "What'd you say?" (earmuffs) / "If you wouldn't mind marrying me too much?" / "Yes. I'd like to marry you." He promises to be good — "I ain't gonna leave no hair in the sink" — then invites the tiger.

The impact

A fixture on greatest-proposal lists, beloved for its awkwardness — no poetry, just Rocky's whole heart in one mumbled sentence.

26Breakfast at Tiffany'sPaul Varjak (George Peppard) → Holly Golightly (Audrey Hepburn)Proposal-adjacent19611960s
The scene

In the rain-soaked alley, Paul produces a ring — fished from a Cracker Jack box and engraved at Tiffany's — and declares his love as they finally stop running from each other. There is no explicit "will you marry me."

The impact

Borderline: no explicit ask. Included for the Cracker Jack ring — one of cinema's most romantic images of devotion on no budget, endlessly referenced.

27The Sound of MusicCaptain Georg von Trapp (Christopher Plummer) → Maria (Julie Andrews)On-screen proposal19651960s
The scene

In the moonlit gazebo, the Captain confesses his engagement to the Baroness is over — he loves someone else. Maria tries to flee; he stops her; they kiss as "Something Good" plays. Afterward he quietly asks from whom he should request permission to marry Maria — understatement as the crest of the scene.

The impact

One of film's most romantic scenes precisely for its restraint; the film won five Oscars including Best Picture and displaced Gone with the Wind as the highest-grossing film ever.

28Love StoryOliver Barrett IV (Ryan O'Neal) → Jennifer "Jenny" Cavilleri (Ali MacGraw)On-screen proposal19701970s
The scene

Driving in Boston after Jenny reveals her Paris scholarship, Oliver blurts it mid-argument: "What about our marriage?" / "Who said anything about marriage?" / "I'm saying it, now." / "You want to marry me?" / "Yeah." / "Why?" / "Because." / "That's a good reason."

The impact

The "Because" exchange distills the film's ethos — love as blunt certainty — in the film that out-grossed every previous Paramount release.

29GigiGaston Lachaille (Louis Jourdan) → Gilberte "Gigi" (Leslie Caron)On-screen proposal19581950s
The scene

After Gigi refuses the mistress arrangement and a disastrous evening at Maxim's shows Gaston that arrangement through her eyes, he wanders Paris all night and returns at dawn to humbly ask for Gigi's hand in marriage — the one thing the lifelong bachelor swore he'd never do.

The impact

The moral climax of a nine-time Oscar winner including Best Picture — love defeating Parisian cynicism.

30Funny GirlNick Arnstein (Omar Sharif) → Fanny Brice (Barbra Streisand) — at her promptingOn-screen proposal19681960s
The scene

Aboard the Europe-bound ship, Fanny engineers it in her comic idiom — where she comes from, when two people "sort of love each other," one says, "Why don't we get married?" Nick takes the line and makes it real; his poker win launches "Sadie, Sadie, married lady."

The impact

Fanny practically proposes to herself and Nick gallantly formalizes it — her comic agency distilled — in Streisand's Oscar-winning debut.

31My Best Friend's WeddingJulianne Potter (Julia Roberts) → Michael O'Neal (Dermot Mulroney) — rejectedOn-screen proposal19971990s
The scene

"Michael, I love you. I've loved you for nine years. I've just been too arrogant and scared to realize it… I really have this gigantic favor to ask of you. Choose me. Marry me. Let me make you happy. Oh, that sounds like three favors, doesn't it?" He marries Kimmy anyway.

The impact

The great rejected proposal of 90s rom-coms — its "Choose me. Marry me." DNA echoes all the way to Grey's Anatomy's "Pick me. Choose me. Love me."

32Ever AfterPrince Henry (Dougray Scott) → Danielle de Barbarac (Drew Barrymore)On-screen proposal19981990s
The scene

After Danielle frees herself, Henry apologizes, declares his love, and proposes by fitting the recovered slipper onto her foot. She accepts.

The impact

Noted for letting the heroine rescue herself first — the proposal as reward for her agency, not his.

33The Five-Year EngagementViolet Barnes (Emily Blunt) → Tom Solomon (Jason Segel)On-screen proposal20122010s
The scene

Tom proposed first, opening the film; then life stretched the engagement to five years. The reversal comes on a roadside: Violet, tired of waiting for the "right" moment, proposes back with an old ruby ring — followed by an improvised Alamo Square wedding.

The impact

The film flipped the genre's gender script, and the roadside scene is its thesis in miniature: proposals as negotiation, not coronation.

34Mamma Mia!Sam Carmichael (Pierce Brosnan) → Donna Sheridan (Meryl Streep)On-screen proposal20082000s
The scene

As Sophie and Sky's ceremony halts, Sam proposes to Donna before the assembled guests — and the halted wedding becomes theirs.

The impact

The joyous hijacking delivers the film's real payoff — the mother's love story eclipsing the daughter's — and became one of the ABBA musical's most cheered moments.

35DeadpoolWade Wilson (Ryan Reynolds) → Vanessa Carlysle (Morena Baccarin)On-screen proposal20162010s

On Christmas, in their bedroom, Wade — not yet Deadpool — proposes with a Ring Pop, telling Vanessa her crazy matches his. She laughs, she accepts: one unguarded moment before the origin-story violence begins.

The impact

The Ring Pop became an instant pop-culture artifact — the anti-Tiffany ring — setting the tone for a superhero romance built on banter and damage.

36The Twilight Saga: EclipseEdward Cullen (Robert Pattinson) → Bella Swan (Kristen Stewart)On-screen proposal20102010s

Edward kneels and offers his mother's ring, promising her "every day of forever." Bella — who has insisted she wants the opposite of a normal life — accepts the most traditional gesture of all.

The impact

For the Twilight generation this was the proposal; the heirloom detail gave it weight inside the fandom, and it remains the franchise's most replayed romantic beat.

37Walk the LineJohnny Cash (Joaquin Phoenix) → June Carter (Reese Witherspoon)On-screen proposal20052000s

40Sense and SensibilityEdward Ferrars (Hugh Grant) → Elinor Dashwood (Emma Thompson)On-screen proposal19951990s

43The NotebookLon Hammond Jr. (James Marsden) → Allie Hamilton (Rachel McAdams)On-screen proposal20042000s

At a formal dance, Lon cites her parents' permission, proposes, and announces the engagement. Elegant and proper — the expected life, made visible.

A corrective to memory: the film's actual proposal is the obstacle, the gilded cage that gives the love triangle its stakes, in the decade's defining romantic drama.

44SerendipityLars Hammond (John Corbett) → Sara Thomas (Kate Beckinsale)On-screen proposal20012000s
