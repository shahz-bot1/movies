100 Greatest Marriage Proposal Scenes in Cinema

Ranked by emotional power, originality, cinematic craft, cultural impact and lasting audience love — from whispered questions to spectacular public declarations.

Full spoilers, by design. Pivotal romantic scenes and endings are discussed throughout. Proposal-adjacent entries are clearly marked.

01
20032000sOn-screen proposal

Love Actually

Jamie (Colin Firth) → Aurélia (Lúcia Moniz)

The scene

After a courtship conducted across a language barrier, Jamie travels to Marseille, France and, before Aurélia's entire family and a restaurant full of patrons, proposes in halting, painstakingly learned Portuguese. She answers in English: yes.

The impact

Widely regarded as the most beloved proposal in modern romantic comedy — the embodiment of the film's love-transcends-language thesis and a cornerstone of its status as a perennial holiday favorite.

02
19961990sOn-screen proposal

Jerry Maguire

Jerry Maguire (Tom Cruise) → Dorothy Boyd (Renée Zellweger)

The scene

Jerry asks Dorothy to marry him while his life and career are still uncertain; she accepts, and they marry before his professional comeback is secure. The film’s famous support-group reunion comes later, after their marriage is strained: Jerry tells her, "You complete me," and Dorothy answers, "You had me at hello."

The impact

Two of the most quoted lines in film history from a Best Picture nominee; Cuba Gooding Jr. won the Supporting Actor Oscar, and "you complete me" entered the language permanently.

03
20052000sOn-screen proposal

Pride & Prejudice

Fitzwilliam Darcy (Matthew Macfadyen) → Elizabeth Bennet (Keira Knightley)

The scene

The rain-soaked first proposal — a torrent of feeling that insults as much as it confesses — is rejected on grounds of arrogance and interference; the dawn renewal, stripped of pride, is accepted.

The impact

One of period-drama cinema's most iconic scenes; its power is passion colliding with principle, a proposal that fails precisely because of how it's delivered.

20042000sProposal-adjacent

50 First Dates

Henry Roth (Adam Sandler) → Lucy Whitmore (Drew Barrymore)

The scene

The film never shows Henry proposing. Instead, its Arctic epilogue reveals that Henry and Lucy are already married and raising a daughter; Lucy wakes aboard their boat, relearns their life through a videotape, and chooses her family anew that morning.

The impact

The marriage reveal subverts the conventional proposal payoff and reframes commitment around choosing someone every single day — the film’s most enduring romantic idea.

The Proposal

Andrew Paxton (Ryan Reynolds) → Margaret Tate (Sandra Bullock)

The scene

At the office, after the sham engagement collapses, Andrew proposes for real: "Margaret, will you marry me? Because I'd like to date you."

The impact

The "date you" button inverts the entire premise — wanting the beginning, not the shortcut — and became the signature, widely quoted line of one of 2009's biggest rom-com hits.

When Harry Met Sally

Harry Burns (Billy Crystal) → Sally Albright (Meg Ryan)

The scene

On New Year's Eve, Harry catalogues everything he loves about Sally, ending: "when you realize you want to spend the rest of your life with somebody, you want the rest of your life to start as soon as possible." Methodology

The ranking weighs memorability and emotional power first, followed by the originality of the reveal, cinematic execution, cultural impact and audience or critical reception. It spans nine decades, multiple genres and both intimate and extravagant gestures.
